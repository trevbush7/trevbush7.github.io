Mentors

My doctoral training is shaped by complementary mentorship in multi-target herbal pharmacology, systems pharmacology, and PET imaging for PD assessment.

Primary Mentor

My PI and primary scientific home at Yale.

Yung-Chi Cheng

Henry Bronson Professor of Pharmacology

Department of Pharmacology, Yale School of Medicine

Chairman, Consortium for the Globalization of Chinese Medicine (CGCM)

Research Focus

Dr. Cheng is a pioneer in antiviral and anticancer drug development and a global leader in applying rigorous pharmacological frameworks to traditional Chinese and herbal medicines. His laboratory studies and develops novel anti-cancer and anti-viral drugs — spanning hepatocellular carcinoma, pancreatic cancer, colorectal cancer, HBV, HCV, HIV, EBV, and CMV — and is currently exploring systems biology approaches to develop multi-targeted medicines for cancer and other aging-related diseases.

Beyond individual drug programs, Dr. Cheng has spearheaded the vision of WE Medicine — a convergence of Western reductionist pharmacology and Eastern multi-target, polychemical herbal medicine — building the Consortium for the Globalization of Chinese Medicine with partners at the University of Hong Kong, Peking University, Tsinghua University, and other major institutions worldwide. With 457 publications and over 34,000 citations, his influence spans antiviral nucleoside analogues, herbal formulation standardization, and network pharmacology.

Role in My Training

My doctoral work in the Cheng Lab investigates how traditional medicinal herbs treat chronic inflammatory diseases through a polychemical, multi-target, systems pharmacology approach. Dr. Cheng provides mentorship in herbal formulation design, pharmacological validation, CETSA-MS and HDX-MS target identification, interaction metabolomics, and the mechanistic interpretation of polypharmacological and synergistic herbal interventions. Through this work, I aim to contribute to his overarching vision of WE Medicine.

Co-Mentor

Providing complementary expertise in brain PET neuroimaging and neuropharmacology.

Jason Cai

Associate Professor of Radiology and Biomedical Imaging, and of Pharmacology

Wu Tsai Institute · Yale School of Medicine

Research Focus

Dr. Cai's research is focused on developing novel PET imaging probes and methodologies for brain neuroimaging, enabling sensitive and quantitative measurements of neuroactivity and neurochemical processes in living systems. His group integrates animal models, behavioral testing, biomarker development, pharmacology, and multimodal imaging to interrogate questions at the intersection of aging, cognition, psychopharmacology, synaptic biology, and systems neuroscience.

Trained in organic chemistry (PhD, University of Missouri–Columbia) and PET radiochemistry (Carolyn Anderson laboratory, University of Pittsburgh), Dr. Cai joined the Yale PET Center in 2015 and has since built a program at the forefront of brain PET probe development and in vivo neuroimaging pharmacology.

Role in My Training

Dr. Cai contributes expertise in positron emission tomography and neuroimaging pharmacology to my training. His mentorship provides quantitative, in vivo imaging tools for tracking the neurochemical and neuroactivity consequences of herbal medicinal interventions in models of neurodegeneration and chronic neuroinflammation — translating molecular target engagement into living system readouts.

Thesis Committee

Faculty guiding my dissertation through complementary expertise in quantitative proteomics, lipidomics, spatial omics, and systems pharmacology.

Yansheng Liu

Associate Professor

Department of Pharmacology & Yale Cancer Center

Research Focus

Dr. Liu's lab develops quantitative mass spectrometry and bioinformatic frameworks to define proteome dynamics, protein abundance, turnover, PTMs, and phosphoproteomic signaling states. His group integrates DIA-MS, lipidomics, and MALDI-based spatial omics to construct spatially organized multi-omic network models.

Role in My Training

Dr. Liu provides complementary mass spectrometry expertise across proteomics and lipidomics. He guides my development of DIA-MS workflows, PTM and phosphoproteomic analysis, multi-omic bioinformatic integration, and MALDI spatial omics approaches for quantitative and spatial modeling of lipid–protein networks.

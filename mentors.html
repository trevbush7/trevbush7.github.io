Mentors

My doctoral training is shaped by herbal systems pharmacology, positron emission tomography, and mass spectrometry imaging.

Primary Mentor

My PI and primary scientific home at Yale.

Yung-Chi Cheng

Henry Bronson Professor of Pharmacology

Department of Pharmacology, Yale School of Medicine

Chairman, Consortium for the Globalization of Chinese Medicine (CGCM)

Research Focus

Dr. Cheng is a pioneer in antiviral and anticancer drug development, with landmark contributions spanning HBV, HCV, HIV, hepatocellular carcinoma, and pancreatic cancer. His laboratory applies rigorous pharmacological frameworks to traditional Chinese and herbal medicines, investigating multi-target, polychemical interventions for cancer and aging-related diseases. He has championed the vision of WE Medicine — a convergence of Western reductionist pharmacology and Eastern multi-target herbal medicine — building a global consortium with partners at the University of Hong Kong, Peking University, and Tsinghua University. With 457 publications and over 34,000 citations, his influence spans antiviral nucleoside analogues, herbal formulation standardization, and network pharmacology.

Role in My Training

Dr. Cheng provides primary mentorship in herbal formulation design, CETSA-MS and HDX-MS target identification, interaction metabolomics, and the mechanistic interpretation of polypharmacological herbal interventions.

Jason Cai

Associate Professor of Radiology and Biomedical Imaging, and of Pharmacology

Wu Tsai Institute · Yale School of Medicine

Research Focus

Dr. Cai develops novel PET imaging probes and methodologies for brain neuroimaging, enabling sensitive and quantitative measurements of neuroactivity and neurochemical processes in living systems. His group integrates animal models, behavioral testing, biomarker development, pharmacology, and multimodal imaging to interrogate questions at the intersection of aging, cognition, and systems neuroscience. Trained in organic chemistry and PET radiochemistry, he joined the Yale PET Center in 2015 and has built a program at the forefront of brain PET probe development and in vivo neuroimaging pharmacology. His work bridges quantitative neurochemical imaging with synaptic biology to develop sensitive tools for tracking neuroactivity dynamics in neurodegenerative and neuroinflammatory disease.

Role in My Training

Dr. Cai provides in vivo PET imaging expertise for quantitatively tracking the neurochemical and neuroactivity consequences of herbal medicinal interventions in models of neurodegeneration and chronic neuroinflammation.

Thesis Committee

Faculty guiding my dissertation through complementary expertise in quantitative proteomics, lipidomics, spatial omics, and systems pharmacology.

Yansheng Liu

Associate Professor

Department of Pharmacology & Yale Cancer Center

Research Focus

Dr. Liu develops quantitative mass spectrometry and bioinformatic frameworks to define proteome dynamics, including protein abundance, turnover, and post-translational modifications. His group specializes in DIA-MS, lipidomics, and MALDI-based spatial omics to construct spatially organized, multi-omic network models of disease. He integrates phosphoproteomic signaling states with lipid network analysis to map how cellular perturbations propagate across molecular layers. His lab bridges proteomics, lipidomics, and spatial mass spectrometry to generate systems-level views of tissue organization in cancer and complex disease.

Role in My Training

Dr. Liu guides my development of DIA-MS workflows, lipidomics, and MALDI spatial omics approaches for quantitative and spatial modeling of lipid–protein networks in disease.

Mentors

My scientific training is shaped by an interdisciplinary team spanning neural stem cell lipid biology, traditional Chinese medicine and drug development, quantitative proteomics and spatial omics, and systems-level tissue circuitry.

Primary Mentor

My PI and primary scientific home at Yale.

Xiaoai Zhao

Assistant Professor

Departments of Neuroscience and Comparative Medicine, Yale School of Medicine

Research Focus

Dr. Zhao studies how complex lipids regulate neural stem cell function and decline during aging. Her lab dissects how phospholipid and sphingolipid acyl-chain composition shapes organelle function, cellular stress responses, and regenerative capacity in the aging brain.

Her group maps organelle-specific lipidomic changes and investigates how compartmentalized lipid species and lipid-derived metabolites drive functional deterioration in age-related disease.

Role in My Training

Dr. Zhao is my primary mentor and the intellectual anchor for my lipidomics work. She provides expertise in organelle-resolved lipidomics, neural stem cell biology, and mass spectrometry method development, shaping how I interrogate lipid-driven systems physiology.

Thesis Committee

Faculty guiding my dissertation through complementary expertise in herbal pharmacology, quantitative proteomics, and systems modeling.

Yung-Chi Cheng

Henry Bronson Professor of Pharmacology

Department of Pharmacology, Yale School of Medicine

Research Focus

Dr. Cheng is a pioneer in antiviral/anticancer drug development and a global leader in traditional Chinese medicine. His lab applies modern pharmacology, mechanistic quality control, and immuno-oncology frameworks to dissect multi-component herbal medicines such as PHY906/YIV-906.

Role in My Training

Dr. Cheng anchors the herbal pharmacology and translational components of my work, offering mentorship in multi-herb formulation design, quality control, and mechanism-based analysis of polypharmacological interventions.

Yansheng Liu

Associate Professor

Department of Pharmacology & Yale Cancer Center

Research Focus

Dr. Liu’s lab defines quantitative rules of proteome dynamics using innovations like DeltaSILAC. His group develops DIA-MS workflows, MALDI imaging, and computational pipelines to map turnover, PTMs, and spatially organized proteomic networks.

Role in My Training

Dr. Liu guides my integration of lipidomics with quantitative proteomics, PTM analysis, and MALDI spatial omics, helping translate lipid-centric findings into quantitative proteostasis and circuit-level models.

Hattie Chung

Assistant Professor

Department of Pathology & Center for Systems Biology, Harvard Medical School

Research Focus

Dr. Chung develops predictive models of tissue biology using spatial genomics, multi-omics, and machine-learning approaches to define cell–cell circuit motifs governing resilience and failure.

Role in My Training

Dr. Chung contributes systems-biology thinking, spatial modeling, and circuit-level frameworks that help me interpret multi-omics data in terms of tissue-level organization and resilience.

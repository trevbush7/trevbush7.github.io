Research Interests

My work is grounded in network pharmacology: mapping how lipid and protein networks become dysregulated in aging and chronic disease, and developing polypharmacological strategies to restore system-level organization.

1. Systems Biology

Multi-omic Network Disorganization

I integrate mass spectrometry–based lipidomics, proteomics, and transcriptomics to identify enzymes, signaling nodes, and molecular modules that drive network-level dysfunction across cells and tissues.

  • Quantitative lipidomics to map complex lipid remodeling
  • Proteomics to define abundance, turnover, and PTMs
  • Transcriptomics to contextualize regulatory shifts
  • Cross-omic integration to infer enzymatic dysregulation

The goal is to move beyond single targets and instead define disease as a state of network miswiring.

2. Polypharmacology

Herbal Network Modulation

Building on these network maps, I pursue a polypharmacological approach using pleiotropic natural products and synergistic combinations derived from herbal medicine.

  • Identification of multi-target natural products
  • Quantification of synergistic compound interactions
  • Latent evolutionary optimization concepts
  • Synthesis-aware prioritization of lead candidates

Rather than suppressing a single protein, this strategy aims to reshape entire lipid–protein circuits toward healthier states.

3. Bioinformatics & Predictive Pipelines

Underlying both research themes is the development of rigorous computational infrastructure for large-scale data analysis, network modeling, and predictive prioritization.

  • Reproducible multi-omic workflows
  • Network inference and pathway modeling
  • Compound–target ranking systems
  • Prediction-driven experimental design

These pipelines transform high-dimensional datasets into actionable, mechanistically grounded therapeutic hypotheses.
